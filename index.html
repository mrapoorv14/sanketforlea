Chapter 03 — what comes back

Nine kinds of answer, one chat.

Every report opens with findings in plain English and closes with what to do next. Send a file, or a command and then a file.

CDR analysis

The subject, their circle and their pattern of life, read out of the call records.

  • Closest associates, ranked by how much contact there was
  • Home, work and last known location — address or coordinates
  • Night stay per night, and nights away from home
  • Call chains, money trail, impossible travel (possible cloned SIM)
  • Handset changes, silent periods, burners, new contacts

IPDR analysis

One page for the IO, then the internet record rebuilt into something readable.

  • WhatsApp and app calls — voice, video, unanswered
  • Apps in plain English; VPN use, including Cloudflare WARP
  • Places, night stay, daily routine, phone-off gaps
  • The subject's own IP addresses, to match against a platform's own logs
  • Several monthly files of one number read as one report

Cross-linkage

/cross

Two to ten files read together, with a link diagram and one sentence per link.

  • Direct calls, same tower at the same time, go-betweens
  • Shared handset, SIM or connection
  • Strength rated Strong, Moderate or Weak
  • A CDR and an IPDR of one number read as a pair

Incident check

/incident DD-MM-YYYY HH:MM

Give it a time and a place and the report opens on that moment.

  • Where the phone was, and how far from the scene
  • Everything it did within two hours either side
  • Whether it went unusually quiet, and first-ever contacts

Tower dumps

/dump … /done

Every operator's dump for every scene, compared in one pass.

  • Numbers present at two or more scenes, with the times at each
  • The same handset carrying a different SIM
  • Calls among those numbers; a common contact who stayed away

Google Earth map

map

A KMZ of the report's geography — free with every report that has placeable towers.

  • Key places: night-stay home, workplace, first and last located
  • Every tower site, each cell's counts and the antenna direction
  • The route by day, plus a track for the time slider

Tower dump plan

towers <lat,long>

Which dumps are actually worth asking for, from the operators' own cell charts.

  • Facing cells of the three nearest sites — request these first
  • The next ring of sites — also worth asking for
  • An Excel of every CGI, with distance and bearing

Platform IP log

Login and activity IP logs returned by a service provider, read into something usable.

  • Where the logins came from — operator, VPN, Tor, abroad
  • A ready-made request list per operator, in IST and UTC
  • Missing-port warnings on shared mobile IPv4; two-places-at-once

Contact summary

For "max callers" sheets that carry one row per contact.

  • Top contacts, ranked by calls and talk time
  • A note on requesting the full CDR for times, towers and night activity

Chapter 05 — the responsibility

Case data stays with the case.

Sanket is for official investigative use, by authorised officers, on data lawfully obtained for a specific case. The handling is written down, not implied.

Local by design

Analysis and IP-ownership lookups run on the host. No CDR or IPDR content is sent to any third party — only the report, to the officer who asked for it.

The panel is localhost-only

It answers on 127.0.0.1 and nowhere else. The password is hashed with scrypt, and five wrong attempts lock sign-in for fifteen minutes.

Access is per number

Only whitelisted officers can submit a file. The operator grants, revokes and blocks access from the panel, officer by officer.

Automatic purge

Uploaded files, reports and their index entries are deleted after the retention period — 30 days by default, shorter if you set it. Delete a report and the operator files behind it go with it.

A permanent audit trail

Every submission, delivery, rejection, block and payment is logged with a timestamp and exports as CSV. In the long-term archive the case data itself is redacted — who received what, and when, is kept.

Every report is marked

Reports carry CONFIDENTIAL, the subject and the case reference, so a printed or forwarded copy is still identifiable case material.

Operator duties — full-disk encryption on the host, off-machine backups, a current access list — are set out in the compliance note shipped with the tool. That note is an operational record, not legal advice: obtain sign-off from your competent authority before deployment.

For the operator

One panel for officers, billing and the log.

A local web panel on the machine that runs Sanket: run a report by hand, watch the WhatsApp line, manage agencies and plans, and search any part of a number across every case the unit has run.

  • Agency tiles, officers by plan, and a drawer per officer — plan, payments, reports, district
  • Billing: revenue, payments with void, usage, prices, UPI
  • Activity: reports, number search, numbers linked across the unit's own cases, the audit log
  • Alerts: broadcast a message, keep a watchlist
